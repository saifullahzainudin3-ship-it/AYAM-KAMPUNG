Derivatives $f'(x)$ in Poultry Weight Gain

Visualizing the instantaneous growth rate of Malaysian Ayam Kampung

The Bioscience Scenario

In Malaysian poultry farming, monitoring the growth trajectory of indigenous Ayam Kampung is crucial for optimizing feed formulation and determining the precise market harvest window (typically between 12 to 16 weeks).

While the weight function $f(x)$ tells us how heavy the chicken is on day $x$, the derivative $f'(x)$ yields the instantaneous rate of mass accumulation (grams per day). Pinpointing when $f'(x)$ peaks tells livestock managers exactly when the bird is growing fastest.

Data Reference: Growth curves adapted from biological baselines published in the Malaysian Journal of Animal Science (MJAS) and MARDI research parameters for indigenous poultry lines.

Mathematical Modeling

Weight Function $f(x)$ [Gompertz-approximated]:
$$f(x) = 1500 \cdot e^{-e^{-0.035 \cdot (x - 45)}}$$
Yields total accumulated weight in grams at day $x$.
Derivative $f'(x)$ (Growth Rate):
$$f'(x) = 52.5 \cdot e^{-0.035(x-45) - e^{-0.035(x-45)}}$$
Calculates the precise weight gain rate in grams/day at day $x$.
